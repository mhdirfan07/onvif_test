OnvifCam

How it works & how the components communicate

1. Architecture — Who Talks to Whom

OnvifCam is made of three connected parts: Flutter as the control panel, the Android Service that does all the heavy work (camera, encoder, RTSP server), and Blue Iris, which watches the result. Each pair talks over whichever channel fits the job.

  • Flutter UI — sends commands over a MethodChannel and receives status/logs over an EventChannel. It does none of the actual work.
  • Android Service — opens the camera, encodes the video, and serves Blue Iris's requests. It runs as a foreground service, so it stays active even with the screen off.
  • Blue Iris — just needs one RTSP address (phone's IP + port 8554) to pull the stream.

2. Video Pipeline — From Camera to Stream

Every frame goes through four stages before it reaches Blue Iris: captured by the camera, compressed to H.264 by the encoder, split into RTP packets, then sent out over the RTSP server. This repeats dozens of times a second for as long as the server is running.

Camera → H.264 Encoder → RTP Packets → RTSP Server → Blue Iris

3. How They Communicate — The RTSP Conversation

Everything below happens over a single TCP connection to port 8554. Blue Iris first asks which methods are supported, is then asked to log in, negotiates how the video will be delivered, and finally triggers a video stream that keeps flowing continuously.

  • The first DESCRIBE always gets a 401 — that's not an error, it's just how Digest authentication asks for login.
  • The SDP reply carries key info (SPS/PPS) so Blue Iris knows how to decode the H.264 video.
  • As soon as PLAY is received, the encoder immediately produces one full keyframe so the picture appears whole right away, with no wait.
This whole conversation happens over a single TCP connection to port 8554.
